03 / Cafe

Hours lived in a text thread. The page had to answer from the passenger seat.

A fictional neighborhood cafe on Main Street in Gainesville, a short hop from Oakwood. Fixed price, one marketing site, built for someone checking the board on the way over.

Role

Web design, content, front-end build

Scope

Mobile-first marketing site, menu, hours, location

Timeline

4 weeks, fixed-price concept engagement

Stack

HTML/CSS, deployed on Vercel

01 — Context

The situation

Maple & Main pours a short menu for people who already live nearby — downtown Gainesville, and the drive in from Oakwood and Mundy Mill. The shop's reputation moved by word of mouth. The website was a desktop template with a stock hero, a PDF menu, and hours that sat below a fold a phone never reached.

Most visits were decided in a car or on a sidewalk. If the page could not say open or closed, what was on the board, and where to park, the person kept driving.

02 — Problem

What was actually broken

  • Hours lived in a text thread — Open, close, and holiday changes were answered one message at a time instead of on the page.
  • The menu was a PDF — A file that pinched, failed, or never finished loading for someone already in the car.
  • No first-screen directions — Street, lot, and "ten minutes from Oakwood" were missing from the only screen a phone shows without scrolling.
  • Desktop-first in a cupholder business — The template assumed a laptop. The session was a phone at a red light on the way from Oakwood.

03 — Approach

How the work was shaped

The scope stayed a marketing site. No online ordering, no account, no catalog. The page has one job: get someone from Oakwood or downtown to a chair by the window.

  • Phone first, then the desktop — Hours, a priced short menu, and a tap to call or open the map sit in the first screen. The story of the maple on Main comes after.
  • A menu you can read at a light — Six items, named and priced. No PDF, no pinch-to-zoom, no "see the board in store."
  • Navy and gold that belong to the shop — #242F40 and #CCA43B, cream paper, type that still reads in sun on a phone. Not a theme picked from a template.
  • Fixed price, written down first — One page, one scope, a number agreed before layout. The engagement does not grow a checkout because the sale is the walk-in.

04 — Decisions

Three calls worth defending

  • No online ordering — The higher-margin sale is the person who sits down. A cart would have spent the budget on the wrong transaction.
  • Hours and a call button before the story — The maple on Main is a good paragraph. It is not what someone needs at 7:10 a.m. on the way from Oakwood.
  • The note form tells the truth — This is a fictional cafe. The form says nothing is delivered, instead of pretending a demo inbox is a host stand.

05 — System

Color and type

Archivo for the name on the window and the menu prices. Mono for hours and the address — the lines a person photographs and sends to the group chat.

06 — Outcome

What the engagement delivers

1 screen

Hours, a priced menu, and directions before the scroll

6

Drinks and pastries, named and priced — not a PDF

Phone

Layout written for a cupholder, then checked on a desktop

Fixed

Scope and price set before the first layout

The engagement produced a marketing site that answers three questions before the light turns green: are you open, what do you pour, and where do I park. The live demo is the page, not a mock in a slide.

Reflection

The temptation on a cafe project is to spend the budget on atmosphere. The atmosphere was already in the room. The page had to inherit it and then get out of the way — hours, menu, map — for someone driving in from Oakwood.
